## What that means in practice

**You may**, without asking anyone:

- study, modify, and redistribute the source;
- use Vey inside your company or your own projects;
- build products on top of Vey and sell them, as long as they are not
  themselves a competing Vey-like decision product or service;
- use it for research and evaluation in any setting;
- self-host it for your own operations.

**You may not, during the two-year protection period:**

- offer a product or service whose value derives substantially from
  Vey's functionality and which competes with the product or service
  the copyright holder offers using it.

Commercial licenses are available for Competing Use, OEM arrangements,
redistribution arrangements that require terms beyond FSL-1.1-MIT, and other
custom commercial terms.

## Two-year MIT conversion

Every version of Vey becomes available under the plain MIT license
two years after its distribution date. The protection period is a
window, not a permanent restriction.

## Model weights

Vey model weights released from the v2.0.0-rc3 licensing boundary forward are
licensed under FSL-1.1-MIT, the same grant as the code; the weights repository
carries its own copy of the license. Weight revisions previously distributed
under AGPL-3.0 retain that historical grant. Commercial terms beyond
FSL-1.1-MIT are available separately.

## Fair Source, not OSI open source

During the protection period Vey is source-available under the FSL,
which is a Fair Source license, not an OSI-approved open source
license. After conversion, the version is plain MIT and fully open
source. We describe Vey accordingly: Fair Source now, MIT later.

## Older releases

Releases distributed under AGPL-3.0 remain available under AGPL-3.0.
The license change applies from the release that carries this notice
forward and does not revoke any grant already made.

## Commercial licensing

Commercial licenses are available for Competing Use, OEM arrangements,
redistribution arrangements that require terms beyond FSL-1.1-MIT, and other
custom commercial terms. Open a GitHub issue with the title "Commercial
license inquiry" to start.

## Contributions

By submitting a contribution you certify that you have the right to
submit it under FSL-1.1-MIT, and you grant the Vey project the right
to include it under FSL-1.1-MIT, under the MIT license once a version
converts, and under the project's commercial license. This is what
lets the project keep a single codebase across all three.
